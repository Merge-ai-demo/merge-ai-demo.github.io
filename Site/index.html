How it works, step by step

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Merge is free, and so are the AIs it installs: open AIs, free to use, made by companies and research labs such as Mistral AI, Google or IBM. No subscription, no ads.

Will it run on my computer?

Merge runs on a Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later (Apple chip or Intel), and on a PC with Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit, Intel or AMD processor). When you open Merge, it looks at your computer by itself and tells you, in plain words, what will be quick and what will be slow. 8 GB of memory or more is recommended.

Why does my computer warn me when I open Merge?

Because Merge is a free beta that is not signed yet: on Mac, we haven't paid for Apple's developer program ($99 a year), and on Windows, for a code-signing certificate. So the first time, your computer asks you to confirm that you trust it. How to open it.

What about Linux, iPhone and Android?

Merge starts on Mac and Windows. Linux, iPhone and Android are planned for later updates.

Is it as good as ChatGPT?

No: the big online AIs are still better, especially for hard tasks. A free AI on your computer handles many everyday tasks, and the more memory your computer has, the better the AI it can run. This is a beta: we are still testing how well each AI does.

Do I need the internet?

Only to download Merge and its AI. After that, Merge works offline. To get recent information, turn on “Web”: Merge then looks things up for your AI, with your OK, in the sources you choose (encyclopedia, news, science). Without it, the AI only knows what it learned when it was made.

Can I talk to it?

Yes, on Mac and Windows: click the sound-wave button and talk; it answers with a voice. Your voice is turned into text on your computer and nothing leaves it. A small AI answers faster, so when you say you want to talk, Merge favours a quick one.

Can the AI be wrong?

Yes. Like every AI, it can make mistakes or make things up, and a small AI more often. Check what matters, and don't rely on it for medical, legal or money decisions.

Can I choose another AI?

Yes. Merge suggests one and shows others that fit your computer, with what each one does. You can change it later too. See all the AIs.

How it works

Open, continue, install.

For the curious: what happens after you click Download.

1

You download Merge and open it

A small file arrives in your Downloads. Open it.

2

Merge looks at your computer by itself

The memory, the processor and the free space, read on your computer: nothing is sent anywhere. On the same screen, you can say what you'll use it for (everyday life, studies, coding, work, languages, health, talking out loud, documents, photos), or skip it. Click Continue.

3

Merge suggests your AI, and says exactly what it will do

The AI that should suit your computer best, what it's good at, its download size, and where it will go. Other AIs, the country they come from and another place (an external drive) are under “Other choices”. If you pick an AI that is too big for your computer, Merge warns you clearly before anything happens: it could freeze your computer.

4

You click Install

Merge downloads your AI from Hugging Face, the big public library of open AIs, not through our servers (on Windows, also the small program that runs it, llama.cpp, from GitHub), and checks each file so a damaged one is never used. It saves everything in its own folder: your files, settings and other apps are not touched. If the connection drops, nothing is lost: click Try again to pick up where it stopped.

5

The chat opens by itself

Start chatting. As much as you like.

Where it comes from

In the app, Merge reads the memory (RAM), the chip or processor, the graphics card and the free disk space from the system. On the website, the preview uses an example computer.

The AI files (GGUF format) come straight from Hugging Face, the public library of open AIs, at a fixed version, never through our servers. Each file is checked with its SHA-256 fingerprint. The AI is run by llama.cpp, an open-source engine (MIT license): built into the Mac app, and downloaded from GitHub by the Windows app when you click Install (fingerprint checked too).

Chatting with your AI

In the beta

Your documents, with page numbers

Add a PDF or a Word file of a few pages to your question. The AI is asked to answer from it, to give the page it used, and to tell you when the document doesn't say.

Where it comes from

PDF and Word (.docx) files, and plain text files, are read inside the app, on your computer, with no added library. PDF text is split into pages (Word files have no pages, so the AI gives the file name), and the AI is instructed to cite them: a small AI may not always do it. Scanned PDFs (pictures of pages with no text inside) can't be read yet. In this beta, the AI keeps about 8,000 “tokens” in mind (roughly 5 pages of text): a longer document is refused with a clear message.

Talk with it out loud

Click the sound-wave button and just talk. Small dots listen, move while the AI thinks, then ripple as it answers with a voice, sentence by sentence; tap them to interrupt. Works with every AI Merge installs, on Mac and Windows.

Where it comes from

Your voice is turned into text on your computer (on a Mac, Apple's on-device speech recognition; on Windows, the speech recognition built into Windows), the AI answers as usual, and a voice installed on your computer reads it. Nothing leaves your computer. For a more natural voice, a Mac can use Apple's free “Enhanced” and “Premium” voices.

On a Mac, Apple's speech recognition, set to work on the Mac only; on Windows, the speech recognition built into Windows, which works offline.

In the beta

Settings that fit each AI

Faster or more careful answers, short or long. With Merge's own AI engine, you only see the settings your AI can really use.

Where it comes from

Merge asks the engine what the AI can do (llama.cpp: its chat template and memory size; Ollama: its capabilities) and sends only matching parameters (temperature, max_tokens, reasoning_effort or chat_template_kwargs). With LM Studio or Jan, Merge can only guess from the AI's name, so all the choices stay visible. When a chat is too long for the AI's memory, the oldest messages are left out, and Merge says so.

In the beta

Web search, only if you allow it

Turn on “Web” and choose the kind of sources: all, encyclopedia, news or science and health. Merge looks things up for your AI, and the answer shows its sources. Only a few search words leave your computer.

Where it comes from

The AI never goes on the internet by itself. Your AI first turns your question into a few English search words; Merge sends only those words, from your computer, to the sources you chose: the Wikipedia API, the GDELT Project's news API (headlines of the last 7 days, without the articles), PubMed (the US National Library of Medicine's database of medical and life-science articles) and OpenAlex (an open catalogue of scientific publications). All are official, free, and need no account. The results are given to your AI with your question, and Merge asks it to treat them as text to read, not as orders. If your AI can't pick search words, Merge doesn't search: your question itself is never sent. No key, no account, nothing goes through our servers. Each search is listed in “What leaves this device”.

Who we are

Good AI can be free. It should stay free.

Why Merge exists, how it works, and where it is going.

Hi, I'm Matthew

I'm a student with a passion for new technology and artificial intelligence, and I'm building Merge. It started with something I kept seeing: people paying for an AI every month, or putting up with limits and ads, without knowing that excellent AIs are now free, open, and able to run on the computer they already own.

You don't need AI every day. But there are moments when it makes a real difference: a letter to write, a document to understand, a lesson to revise, a question late at night. My goal is simple: put that help within everyone's reach, whatever their budget and whatever their device.

What you can count on today

  • Two clicks to install your AI, once Merge is open. Merge tells you what it will download, and where, before anything happens.
  • 20 free AIs it knows how to install on a Mac, 32 on a Windows PC, from 10 makers, among them Mistral AI, Google, IBM, Microsoft and OpenAI.
  • No account, no ads, no message limit. Only your computer's speed sets the pace.
  • Your chats and your files stay on your computer. What does leave it, and when, is listed in Privacy.

What Merge does, and what it doesn't

Merge doesn't build AIs. Labs and research teams around the world publish open AIs that anyone may use, each under its own license. Merge finds the one that should suit your device and your needs, by our honest estimate, installs it, then gets out of the way. You can always choose another one, and see who made each AI and where it comes from.

How Merge pays for itself

Today, it doesn't. Merge is completely free: no subscription, no ads, no account needed, no message limit. Later, we plan to offer optional things you pay for once, such as the home box described below, never a subscription. The plan is for the app to stay free.

A beta, said plainly

This first version is a free beta, for people who like to try things early. It has not been reviewed by outside experts yet. We plan to have lawyers review what Merge promises and how it handles your data, and security experts review the app and its downloads. When they have, we will say who checked what.

Until then, here is what we can say for sure: Merge needs no account, shows no ads, and doesn't send your conversations or your files to us. If something goes wrong, tell us: we read everything.

Where we're going

Merge starts small: one free AI on your Mac or Windows PC. Then, update after update, we plan to add Linux, iPhone and Android, more languages, and the features listed in “Coming in an update”. We will always say clearly what works and what doesn't. Two principles will not change: your AI runs on hardware you own, and nothing leaves it without your OK. No subscription, ever.

A personal AI server for your home

Later, we plan for a computer you already have to serve the whole household, each person with their own chats, on their phone too. Then a small box bought once could do the same with a much larger AI, so that even a small phone gets its answers.

Privacy

What leaves your computer.

In short: your conversations and your files stay on your computer. Here is everything, in plain words.

In the Merge app

Your conversations and the files you add stay on your computer. The AI runs there. They are not sent to us or to anyone else.

No account, no ads, no tracking. The app doesn't measure what you do in it, and doesn't send us any statistics.

Downloading the AI. When you click Install, Merge downloads the AI's files straight from Hugging Face, the public library of open AIs, not through us (on Windows, also the AI engine, from GitHub). Like any website, they see your internet address (IP address) when they send the files. See Hugging Face's privacy policy.

Web search, only if you turn it on. When “Web” is on (Merge asks your OK the first time), your AI first turns your question into a few search words, and Merge sends only those words, from your computer, to the sources you chose in the “Web” menu: Wikipedia, the GDELT Project's news database, PubMed and OpenAlex. Like any website, they see the search words and your internet address. See Wikipedia, GDELT, PubMed and OpenAlex. Your chat and your files are never sent.

The internet otherwise. Merge doesn't connect anywhere else, unless you yourself type the address of another AI program in Settings. In the app, Settings › “What leaves this device” lists every connection the chat window makes.

Deleting everything. In Settings, “Delete all chats” erases your conversations, and you can delete the installed AI to free its space. Do that before removing Merge: the AI is a big file kept apart from the app. On Mac, then move Merge to the Trash (a few small settings and log files stay in your Library folder). On Windows, delete the folders %LOCALAPPDATA%\Merge and %APPDATA%\Merge, the “Merge AIs” folder if you put the AIs on another drive, and the “Merge” shortcuts.

On this website

This website has no account, no ads and no tracking tools. The company that hosts it keeps the usual technical logs of visits (internet address, page, time), as every host does.
